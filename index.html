The Brand Breakdown

Ford Australia

The Modern Powerhouse

Strengths: Still manufacturing right-hand drive for Aus, market-leading tech, dominating the ute segment.

Weaknesses: Higher entry price for new models, massive wait times on premium trims.

Best Models: Ranger (Best Ute Australia), Everest, Mustang.

Ideal Buyer: The tradie wanting the best new tech, or the enthusiast buying a brand-new V8.

Holden

The Cult Classic

Strengths: Unmatched legacy, massive aftermarket support, affordable V6 options, appreciating V8s.

Weaknesses: No longer producing new cars, parts for niche models are becoming scarce.

Best Models: Commodore (VE/VF), Colorado, Monaro.

Ideal Buyer: First car buyers (P-platers), budget-conscious families, and collectors wanting a piece of history.

The Details That Matter

Which is more reliable in Australia?

If we’re talking modern, factory-backed reliability, Ford takes the crown simply because you can still buy them new with a 5-year unlimited kilometre warranty. The latest Ranger and Everest platforms have proven remarkably sturdy under harsh Aussie conditions. However, if you are looking at the used market, Holden’s VF Commodore is legendary. The Alloytec V6 and LS V8 engines are bulletproof if serviced right, and literally every mechanic from Sydney to the Simpson Desert knows how to fix them.

Conclusion: Ford for new-car peace of mind; Holden for raw, fix-it-with-a-hammer durability.

Best for P-Platers?

For P-platers, budget and insurance premiums rule the day. Holden is arguably the king here. A well-maintained used Holden Cruze, Astra, or a V6 Commodore (check local power-to-weight P-plate laws) provides cheap entry into the car scene with dirt-cheap spare parts at Supercheap Auto. Ford answers back with the Fiesta and Focus, which are excellent, zippy hatchbacks, but parts can sometimes be pricier.

Conclusion: Holden offers more affordable, easily modifiable options for young drivers.

Which is better for performance?

The Bathurst rivalry lives on. If you want rear-wheel-drive V8 fun today, the Ford Mustang is basically the only affordable brand-new option left. It’s sharp, modern, and handles incredibly well. Conversely, an SS Commodore (Holden) is a raw, brute-force muscle car that seats five. While Ford wins the current track times with the new Mustang Dark Horse, the visceral feeling of a cammed Holden LS3 is hard to beat.

Conclusion: Ford for modern track precision; Holden for pure, unapologetic muscle.
